Walking — Moving Low-Rate Impacts with Harmonics

Walking appears as a diagonal track of quasi-periodic footfalls that traverse channels with a 1–3 Hz step rate and low-frequency dominance.
Signature summary. Temporal envelope: pulse train; typical pass 3–20 s depending on speed. Recurrence: cadence ≈1–3 Hz (adult gait); visible harmonics up to tens of Hz. Spectral bands: strongest < 30–60 Hz; site-dependent. Spatial spread: coherent motion across adjacent channels; velocity proxy from slope (m/s ≈ channel spacing / time between channel maxima). Amplitude stats: moderate RMS; kurtosis moderate (impacts weaker than digging).

Differentiators. Vs. digging/knocking: shows lateral movement and regular cadence; impacts are weaker. Vs. vehicles: much higher group velocity and broader spatial footprint for vehicles; richer low-frequency content.
Operational context & false positives. Jogging/running increases cadence (2–4 Hz) and amplitude; groups produce multiple parallel tracks. Use step-rate detection (autocorrelation/zero-crossing), inter-channel coherence, and slope-based velocity to confirm.
